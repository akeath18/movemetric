Support

How the numbers work, the things that usually go wrong, and how to reach a person.

01

Getting help

Email support@movemetric.org. One person reads that inbox, and you will normally hear back within two business days. During the school year, replies may take a little longer.

To make a bug report useful, include:

  • Your iPhone model and iOS version
  • The app version, from Settings at the bottom of the screen
  • What you did, what you expected, and what happened instead
  • A screenshot, if it is something you can see

Please do not email screenshots of anything you would not want in an inbox. A description of the problem is almost always enough.

02

The first two weeks

Your targets are meant to look approximate at first. Basal starts from a textbook formula, which is a population average and is wrong for most individuals by a few hundred calories a day. It then spends about two weeks comparing what your weight should have done against what it actually did, and corrects itself.

So the number you see on day one is a starting guess. The number you see in week four is yours. Trends shows how far the correction has moved and why.

To let it learn, it needs two things: food logged on most days, and weigh-ins. Daily weigh-ins are best — not because any single morning matters, but because the app uses a seven-day average and an average needs numbers to average.

03

Common problems

  • A meal was read wrong. Tap it. Every item, amount and number is editable, and every assumption the parser made is spelled out. You can also tap "Look these numbers up again" after changing the amount, rather than doing the arithmetic yourself.
  • Health data is missing. Open the Health app, then Data Access & Devices, then Basal, and confirm it is allowed to read what it asks for. The app re-checks every time you open it, so a permission you grant there appears straight away.
  • My weight looks out of date. Settings shows where the number came from and when. Basal always uses the most recent weigh-in, whether that came from your scale through Apple Health or from you typing it.
  • Today's target changed and I don't know why. Tap the note above the ring. It lists every day that went into the calculation, what each contributed, and which days were left out for having nothing logged.
  • I missed a day. Tap the calendar icon on Today. A day with nothing logged is treated as missing data, never as a day you ate nothing, and it is left out of your targets entirely until you fill it in.
  • I logged something with no signal. It is kept. When you are back online the macros are worked out and the meal waits under "ready to check" on Today, dated to when you typed it.
  • Carbs show "none" and no bar. That is a zero-carb plan working as intended. A progress bar against a target of zero can only ever look like failure, so the app shows the count instead.
  • The widget says "Open Basal". It shows the last day the app worked out. Open the app once and it fills in.
  • Reminders are not arriving. Check Settings, then Notifications, then Basal. If you declined the prompt, iOS will not ask again — turn them on there. The Today screen still shows when a weigh-in is due either way.
04

More than one device

Your log syncs through your own iCloud account. Sign in to iCloud on both devices with the same Apple Account, install Basal on each, and they keep in step on their own. There is nothing to set up and no account to create.

If one device is not catching up: check that both are signed in to the same iCloud account, that iCloud is enabled for Basal under your name in the Settings app, and that the device has a connection. Sync is not instant — it arrives within a minute or so, usually faster.

Signing in with Apple is optional and is not what makes sync work. You can use Basal across all your devices without ever signing in.

05

Getting your data out, or deleting it

Export. Settings → Export my data writes your meals, days and weigh-ins to CSV files you can open in any spreadsheet. Nothing about the format is ours; you can take it anywhere.

Delete. Settings → Delete all data removes everything from the phone, clears the synced copy on your other devices, and deletes the meals and weights Basal wrote to Apple Health. If you signed in, Settings → Account → Delete account and data does all of that and signs you out.

We hold nothing, so there is nothing to ask us to delete — see the privacy policy for exactly what that means.

06

A word about what this is for

Basal is a tracker, not a clinician, and not a medical device. It will not set a target below your resting metabolic rate and it caps weight loss at 1% of bodyweight per week, but those are guard rails rather than supervision.

If you have a health condition that eating patterns affect, if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, or if you have a history of disordered eating, please talk to a professional who knows your situation before acting on anything the app suggests. If counting is making things worse rather than better, the right move is to stop counting.
